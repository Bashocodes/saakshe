FAQ

How it works, in six questions.

What is saakshe?

saakshe is the agentic company you talk to. You speak only to one witness — saakshe — in chat and voice. Behind that single front door, a four-faculty agentic company runs the work: manas knows (memory & grounding), arivu decides (five lenses debate, a Claude bench seals and stress-tests the verdict), kalai makes (brand-checked creative), and kural engages (the only mouth, holding the publish gate).

How does the flywheel work?

It's one resumable, two-gate loop: connect your project → manas grounds it in your own memory → you ask a real decision and arivu decides at GATE 1 (your tap) → kalai makes and kural drafts → you publish at GATE 2 (your tap) → manas learns the outcome and the wheel turns again.

Nothing irreversible happens without your tap. Resumable means the run carries its state forward, so when you approve a gate it continues exactly where it paused.

What do credits buy — and how many are free?

Every AI action spends credits: a flywheel run is about 20, connect + ingest about 20, a manas edit about 10, a kalai make about 15, a kural engage about 15 (indicative, tuned during launch). You get 100 free credits on sign-up — roughly 5 full decision runs on the house. You're never charged for a run that fails. See the pricing page for the full table.

Is my data private?

Yes. Your connected sources are read-only — granted, never taken — and we don't sell your data or train public models on it. AI processing runs on Google Gemini via Vertex AI; auth and storage are on Supabase. Full detail, retention, and deletion-on-request are in the Privacy Policy.

How do I connect my project?

Sign in with Google, then connect a repo and a site you control over setu — saakshe's read-only connector. manas ingests them into a cited Context Pack, and from that moment the witness can answer from your own memory. You bring your own keys; saakshe never holds the credentials that move money or post for you.

Does the AI really decide?

It deliberates, then waits for you. In arivu, five lenses (econ, growth, brand, risk, ops) argue the question, then a Claude bench seals the verdict and an adversarial prosecutor tries to defeat it against a defensibility bar. But the chain halts at your tap — nothing irreversible happens without you. The witness only reports what it can see, and refuses beyond it.
